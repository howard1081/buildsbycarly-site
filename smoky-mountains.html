Smoky Mountains

Great Smoky Mountains National Park

The Great Smoky Mountains National Park is a majestic natural wonder, renowned for its diverse wildlife, rich history, and stunning landscapes. This vast wilderness invites exploration with its hiking trails, scenic viewpoints, and tranquil streams. Ideal for adventurers, nature lovers, and those seeking a peaceful retreat, the Smokies offer an unparalleled connection with the great outdoors.

Landmarks and attractions

Landmark 01 of 08

Clingman's Dome

Clingmans Dome, the highest point in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, offers breathtaking panoramic views that are truly unmatched. This renowned spot invites visitors to ascend its observation tower and witness the stunning vistas of the Smokies. Ideal for hikers, photographers, and nature lovers, the journey to Clingmans Dome is as enchanting as the destination itself, with beautiful trails winding through lush forests. It's more than just a viewpoint; Clingmans Dome is an unforgettable experience that encapsulates the majestic beauty of the mountains.

Visit Clingman's Dome
Landmark 02 of 08

Cades Cove

Cades Cove in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park offers a serene and picturesque glimpse into the region's historical and natural beauty. This beloved valley is a haven for wildlife, historical structures, and breathtaking landscapes. Visitors can explore the area by car, bike, or on foot, encountering historic homesteads, grazing deer, and stunning mountain backdrops. Cades Cove isn't just a scenic drive; it's a tranquil escape that connects you with the heritage and untouched beauty of the Smokies.

Visit Cades Cove
Landmark 03 of 08

MOUNT LeCONTE

Mt. LeConte, one of the highest peaks in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, offers a truly rewarding experience for avid hikers and nature lovers. With trails that showcase stunning vistas, diverse flora, and often a glimpse of local wildlife, it's a hiker's paradise. The journey to the summit provides various challenging yet scenic routes, each leading to breathtaking views and the sense of accomplishment that comes with reaching one of the park's most famous landmarks. Mt. LeConte isn't just a hike; it's an adventure that immerses you in the heart of the Smokies' natural splendor.

Visit MOUNT LeCONTE
Landmark 04 of 08

Fontana Lake

Fontana Lake, nestled in the Great Smoky Mountains, offers a tranquil and picturesque setting for water enthusiasts and nature lovers. This vast and pristine lake is ideal for boating, fishing, and swimming, surrounded by lush forests and rolling hills. Visitors can enjoy the peaceful waters, explore secluded coves, or simply relax and take in the stunning mountain views. Fontana Lake is more than just a body of water; it's a serene escape where the beauty of nature and the joy of outdoor activities combine in perfect harmony.

Visit Fontana Lake
Landmark 05 of 08

Horseback Riding

Horseback riding in the Smoky Mountains provides a unique and memorable way to explore the area's natural beauty. From leisurely trail rides through quiet forests to more adventurous treks across rolling hills, horseback riding offers a different perspective of the Smokies. It's an activity that connects you with nature, allowing for peaceful encounters with the region's diverse flora and fauna. Whether you're a seasoned rider or trying it for the first time, horseback riding in the Smokies is an experience that combines adventure, relaxation, and the picturesque scenery of Tennessee.

Visit Horseback Riding
Landmark 06 of 08

Newfound Gap

Newfound Gap, straddling the border between Tennessee and North Carolina, offers one of the most scenic drives in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park. This stunning route, rising to an elevation of over 5,000 feet, provides spectacular views of the surrounding mountains and valleys. It's a place where visitors can experience the serene beauty of the highlands, with opportunities for memorable photographs, peaceful hikes, and encountering diverse wildlife. Newfound Gap isn't just a road; it's a journey through some of the most breathtaking landscapes in the Smokies.

Visit Newfound Gap
Landmark 07 of 08

Appalachian Trail

The Appalachian Trail, winding through the Great Smoky Mountains, is a legendary path that offers hikers an immersive experience in one of America's most beautiful landscapes. This iconic trail provides a range of experiences, from challenging ascents to tranquil walks amidst lush greenery. Whether embarking on a short day hike or a longer journey, the Appalachian Trail invites adventurers to explore the rich biodiversity, stunning vistas, and serene backcountry of the Smokies. It's not just a trail; it's an adventure that connects you with the heart of the wilderness and the spirit of exploration.

Visit Appalachian Trail
Landmark 08 of 08

Auto Touring

Auto touring in the Smoky Mountains offers a leisurely way to soak in the breathtaking landscapes from the comfort of your vehicle. Scenic drives like the Cades Cove Loop Road provide stunning views and glimpses of wildlife, perfect for those who love to explore at their own pace. For the more adventurous, ATV rides through the Smokies promise an exhilarating experience, allowing you to traverse rugged trails and discover hidden gems of the mountain terrain. Whether you're behind the wheel of a car or riding an ATV, the Smokies offer an unforgettable journey through some of Tennessee's most captivating scenery.

Visit Auto Touring

Did you know?

Smoky Mountains fun facts

Peak Tourism

The Great Smoky Mountains is the most visited national park in the country.  The Smoky Mountains gets more annual visitors than the Grand Canyon, Yosemite, and Yellowstone combined.

Hiking Trails

The Great Smoky Mountains has more than 800 miles of hiking trails, more than 90 historic structures, and about 2,900 miles of streams, all of which you can fish in

Fireflies

Great Smoky Mountains National Park is one of only two locations in the world where you can witness the synchronous fireflies phenomenon.

Salamander Capital

While the Park may be known better for its black bear population, it is also considered the “Salamander Capital of the World” The Park is home to 24 species of lung-less salamanders.

Appalachian Trail

The Smokies include part of the Appalachian Trail. A 70-mile stretch of the 2,178-mile Appalachian Trail winds through Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

Short Drive

The Great Smoky Mountains National Park is within a day’s drive of 60% of the nation.

Cherokee Roots

The Smokies got their name from the Cherokee people. They called it “Shaconage” (shah-con-ah-jey) which means “place of the blue smoke.” The name comes from the blue mist that floats above the peaks of the mountains.

Privately Funded

The Great Smoky Mountains is the only national park in the United States that was created using private funds. The government wanted to establish a national park in the east, but it didn’t have enough money to purchase land. Local landowners and John D. Rockefeller donated money, with Rockefeller donating about $5 million dollars, creating the Great Smoky Mountains National Park.
